Complaint

Too many price alerts

Alert fatigue is the most common complaint about competitor price monitoring — 200 alerts a morning, most of them nothing. If you've stopped reading your monitor's emails, the monitor has already failed at its one job.

Why this is a problem

Generic page monitors alert on any change to a page, not just the price. A banner rotates, a stock counter ticks down, a cookie popup gets re-rendered, a "last updated" timestamp refreshes — to a pixel-diff tool, all of that looks exactly like a price change. Track 50 SKUs across a handful of competitors this way, and you get a wall of alerts by Thursday.

The predictable outcome: you stop reading them. And once you've trained yourself to skim past the alert emails, the one that actually matters — a real price drop — gets buried with the rest. The monitor is technically running. It has stopped being useful.

How PriceGazer is different

PriceGazer reads the price, not the page. Before comparing anything, it strips out the parts of a page that don't matter — navigation, banners, timestamps, stock counters, cookie notices, scripts — and diffs only the content that does: the price itself, plan names, features, trial terms.

That means one email when a number really moves, not twenty when nothing did. Tracking 50 SKUs shouldn't mean 200 alerts a morning — it should mean silence until something you actually need to know about happens.

The bottom line

If your inbox is why you stopped trusting your price monitor, that's not a you problem — it's what happens when a tool alerts on pixels instead of prices. PriceGazer is built to only tell you what matters. Start watching in two minutes, or read how to track competitor prices properly.
